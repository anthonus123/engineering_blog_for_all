THE SENZU BEAN MENTALITY

Written in February 11, 2025 by Anthony Maalouly Engineering


Like many kids who grew up in the ’90s, I was captivated by the popular anime Dragon Ball and its various spin-offs, created by the late Akira Toriyama. This anime fascinated me for reasons beyond its stunning martial arts and epic battles. Perhaps it was the timeless themes of good versus evil, courage, friendship, redemption, lighthearted humor, or the relentless pursuit of self-improvement by facing increasingly stronger opponents.


When I was younger, one scene remains etched in my memory to this day: it was when Goku, the main character, gave a senzu bean to Cell. In this saga, Cell is the antagonist intent on destroying Planet Earth, while Goku and the Z-fighters do everything in their power to protect it. Driven by a sense of fair play, and perhaps more fittingly mostly fighter’s pride, the Z-fighters took turns battling Cell. While Cell battled Gohan—the strongest warrior at the time and Goku’s son—Goku handed a senzu bean to Cell, an act that seemed unthinkable. In the anime, a senzu bean instantly cures ailments, restoring full health, strength, and stamina. So knowing all of that, why would Goku give a senzu bean to the main enemy that, at that time, was winning all fights and would pose as the biggest threat on earth?


When I first saw this scene, I was totally outraged! It was as if Goku was suicidal, and was sending his son to a certain death. Spoiler alert: strengthening Cell ultimately unlocked Gohan’s hidden fighting potential, paving the way for the Z-fighters’ victory. That unforgettable scene in Dragon Ball wasn’t just entertainment, it was a lesson in growth.

Iron Sharpens Iron

You cannot become stronger unless you are challenged by strong opponents. Tough challenges are important as they push us to become better. When I was doing my undergrad, many times I thought to myself, why was engineering so difficult? Part of the challenge came from some professors who didn’t prioritize delivering high-quality lectures, but another part was the inherent complexity of the concepts themselves. Grasping these ideas on a deep level, combined with the relentless workload throughout the semester, made it an uphill battle. In hindsight, I understand why it was done this way: it made me stronger and more ready to tackle on bigger problems in life.


Today, there is no shortage of tough challenges in engineering: optimising transportation, maintaining and developing critical infrastructure, generating clean energy, managing waste efficiently—the list goes on. So, pick a challenging problem that interests you, with the key word being challenging. If the problem isn’t challenging, you won’t learn as much, and the victory won’t feel as rewarding. The world needs more people working on challenging and important problems. I remind myself that I’d rather attempt a challenging problem—even if I fail—than take the safe route with trivial ones. At least, for me, this brings more satisfaction in the long run.

Winning when your opponent is at their best

This essentially removes all excuses from your opponent, and to me that is where the most satisfying victory lies. You hear it often in sports, “Oh our team lost because we didn’t have this player due to an injury”, for example. Equally important is the experience of losing even when you give your best. Though such defeats are hard to accept, they often pave the way for the greatest growth. This process is essential in becoming the best version of yourself.


When I was in engineering school, I found it difficult, and I think many of my friend did as well. You try really, really hard, and sometimes you still fail. At some point you ask yourself, can I do it? Do I have what it takes? While there are no one shoe fits all answer, all I can say is don’t give up, try harder, and believe in yourself, because if you don’t, who will? This is the growth pain, and what is hard today, will become easy given enough time, effort, and energy. Remember, the first time you learned about quadratic formula, it was probably the longest formula you have seen in your life at that time, but when you see it today, you think to yourself, “Oh okay, it’s not so bad”.


In summary, be like Goku. Embrace challenges and even help your adversaries grow, because every setback is an opportunity for personal strength. Remember, true progress comes from confronting tough problems and learning from failure. We remember those who dared to take risks—why not be one of them?
